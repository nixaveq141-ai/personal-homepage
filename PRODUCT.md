# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fellow students, online friends, and people who share anime/tech interests. They arrive curious — from a GitHub link, a shared post, or a friend's recommendation. Their context is casual browsing, often on mobile between classes or at night on desktop. They want to see what Puearill is about, explore projects and writing, maybe leave a comment or discover shared interests.

The site should make them feel **curious**, **immersed**, and **delighted** — like discovering a hidden room full of interesting things, not scanning a profile.

## Product Purpose

A personal homepage that expresses identity, showcases creative work (projects, articles, photos, music), and builds community through comments and interactions. Success is visitors staying to explore, leaving a comment, or remembering the site as distinctive and genuine.

## Brand Personality

**幻想 · 深邃 · 灵动** (fantastical, deep, dynamic). The site is a personal universe (个人宇宙) — a curated laboratory of projects, thoughts, and creative work. It draws from anime visual language, cyber aesthetics, deep-space atmospherics, and the quiet wonder of fireflies in darkness. The feeling is part workshop, part gallery, part observatory: a space where interesting things orbit with their own gravity.

The voice is friendly and direct — a tech-savvy creator sharing what they love. Visitors should feel like they've stepped into someone's private atelier, not a branded storefront.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate SaaS landing pages**: white backgrounds, blue/purple gradient buttons, hero illustrations, feature grids, "streamline your workflow" copy. Dead on arrival for a personal brand.
- **Social media profile replacements**: Linktree, Carrd, and similar disposable link-bio pages. No depth, no personality, templated feel.
- **Generic developer portfolios**: minimal dark theme, monospace everything, terminal aesthetics, "I build things" tagline. Exchanged one template for another.
- **Minimalist/brutalist personal sites**: hyper-restrained typography, raw HTML aesthetics, deliberately un-designed. This site has atmosphere and depth — it should feel crafted, not stripped.
- **Flat/material design blogs**: no dimensionality, no glow, no spatial feeling. The site needs atmosphere — depth, light, and shadow working together.
- **Terminal/CLI aesthetic sites**: green-on-black, monospace-only, command-line nostalgia. This isn't a terminal; it's a personal universe with warmth, color, and dimensional depth.

## Design Principles

1. **Personal universe, not personal brand.** The site is a cosmos of curated content — projects, articles, music, photos, moments — each orbiting with its own gravity. It's a space to explore, not a resume to scan.

2. **Atmosphere over decoration.** Depth, glow, and spatial feeling create the mood. The site should feel dimensional — deep space with points of light — not flat-decorated. Every visual choice either builds atmosphere or strips it away.

3. **Energy with wonder.** Motion and color evoke curiosity and delight, not urgency. Visitors should want to linger, drawn in by the glow of interesting things. The energy of anime openings, not the urgency of dashboards.

4. **Show, don't tell.** Projects, articles, photos, and music are evidence of who Puearill is. Content does the work. No filler copy.

5. **Real interaction.** Comments and replies make this a living space, not a static billboard. Community features should feel integrated and welcoming, not bolted-on.

6. **Each content type earns its own space.** Six distinct content types (projects, articles, photos, music, moments, about) each deserve their own spatial treatment — card grids for projects, masonry for photos, split layouts for music, vertical feeds for moments. No uniform template.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Good-faith best practices: follow WCAG contrast guidelines, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, use semantic HTML. No formal WCAG compliance target, but accessibility should never be an afterthought — especially for a personal site where the audience may include people with diverse needs.
